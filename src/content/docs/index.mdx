computing. It brings hardware, scheduling, project storage, and reusable
container environments together so research teams can spend less time
assembling infrastructure and more time running experiments.

## Built together at TU/e

Spike is a shared university investment, made possible by EAISI and six TU/e
departments: Mathematics and Computer Science, Electrical Engineering,
Mechanical Engineering, Biomedical Engineering, Industrial Design, and
Industrial Engineering & Innovation Sciences.

For commercial and industry computing on Spike A, dedicated NVIDIA Blackwell
B300 systems are provided through Supercomputing Brabant and hosted and operated
by TU/e as a founding major partner.

That mix matters. Spike supports work across disciplines, from medical imaging
and scientific machine learning to design and engineering research. The TU/e
Supercomputing Center operates the shared platform and helps turn that
collective investment into computing researchers can use.

## What Spike provides

Spike provides shared GPU systems and scheduling, project-scoped data sources,
and container-based environments. Access is organized through projects: the
project you work in determines which data, environments, and compute resources
you can use.

This site covers the researcher-facing path, from getting access to running and
troubleshooting workloads. Credentials, internal identifiers, quota decisions,
and project-administration procedures stay out of the public documentation.
