Classify the complete data flow before copying even a sample to Spike. The
intake decision covers the stated dataset, users, storage, transfer route,
processing, outputs, and retention period. A decision for one dataset does not
automatically approve a later dataset or a new way of using it.

This page is a Spike admission guide, not a replacement for the TU/e Research
Data Management Framework Policy, a Data Management Plan, ethical review, a
data agreement, or privacy assessment. When the classification or approval is
unclear, the data is **not yet approved for Spike**. Keep it at its current
approved location and contact your Data Steward and SCC AI Engineer.

## Start with the data, not its filename

Answer these questions for the source data and for the information produced by
the workload:

1. Does it relate to an identified or identifiable person, directly or when
   combined with other information?
2. Is it anonymized, pseudonymized, directly identifiable, or special-category
   personal data? Removing names alone does not necessarily anonymize data.
3. Is access restricted by an agreement, licence, embargo, intellectual-property
   obligation, export condition, or collaborator requirement?
4. Where will it enter Spike, who needs access, which external services can see
   it, where will results go, and when must each copy be removed?
5. Could prompts, logs, embeddings, model weights, checkpoints, predictions, or
   visualizations reveal source records or other restricted information?

Use the most restrictive applicable answer. A public input does not make every
derived output public, and joining two low-risk datasets can create a more
identifiable or restricted dataset.

## Spike intake outcomes

The descriptions below are operational outcomes for Spike. They are not new
TU/e classification labels.

| Dataset situation | Spike outcome | What must be established before transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open or published non-personal data | **Normal intake** | Owner or source, licence, integrity/version, expected size, and retention period |
| Restricted or confidential non-personal research data | **Documented approval** | Data owner, access list, contractual or licence restrictions, approved transfer and storage, and permitted outputs |
| Anonymized research data | **Documented approval** | How anonymization was performed, residual re-identification risk, source owner, and confirmation that no key or reasonably available information can restore identity |
| Pseudonymized personal data | **Stop and review** | A Data Steward decision, applicable ethical/privacy approvals, purpose and legal basis, key custody outside the dataset, named users, and SCC confirmation that the required controls are available |
| Directly identifiable personal data, unreviewed special-category personal data, or direct participant/end-user processing | **Do not transfer through normal onboarding** | Ask the Data Steward and SCC to identify an approved platform and workflow. Do not use Spike unless a specific, documented decision authorizes the complete data flow |
| Passwords, API keys, private keys, tokens, or a pseudonymization key | **Never upload as project data** | Use the approved secret mechanism. Keep a pseudonymization key in a different approved location from the dataset |

Pseudonymized data remains personal data. Anonymization must be effective for
the actual data and context; calling a dataset "anonymous" is not evidence.

## Evidence to bring to intake

Give SCC enough information to verify the decision without placing sensitive
content in email, Jira, chat, or a support ticket:

- a plain-language description of the data and research purpose;
- the data owner, project lead, and Data Steward contact;
- whether the data is non-personal, anonymized, pseudonymized, directly
  identifiable, or special-category personal data;
- the approval or decision reference for the DMP, ethical review, privacy
  assessment, data agreement, and any owner restrictions that apply;
- the current location, expected size, source organization, transfer route,
  Spike destination, and intended output destination;
- the complete named user list, external collaborators, and required access
  period;
- the anonymization or pseudonymization method and, where applicable, who holds
  the key and where it is kept;
- approved external services, model endpoints, experiment trackers, cloud
  storage, or other systems that could receive data or derived information; and
- the retention, archival, and deletion plan for inputs, intermediate files,
  checkpoints, models, logs, and results.

Link to approvals held in their authoritative restricted system. Do not attach
raw participant data, secrets, pseudonymization keys, or unnecessary sensitive
details to the intake record.

## Rules after approval

An approved classification becomes part of the project boundary:

- use only the approved project storage, datasource, transfer route, and named
  users;
- keep a pseudonymization key separate from the dataset and from ordinary
  workload access;
- do not place sensitive values in container images, Git repositories,
  commands, environment dumps, logs, screenshots, notebooks, or support cases;
- treat prompts, extracts, embeddings, checkpoints, model weights, predictions,
  and logs as at least as restricted as their source until the owner or Data
  Steward confirms otherwise;
- do not send data or derived information to an external cloud service,
  experiment tracker, model endpoint, or collaborator unless that flow is part
  of the approval; and
- verify the destination and a small non-sensitive test before starting the
  real transfer.

Request a new review before adding a dataset, linking datasets, changing the
purpose, adding users, enabling external egress, hosting a model, exporting
results, or changing retention. Stop work and contact SCC immediately if data
arrives outside the approved boundary or is exposed to the wrong user or
service.

## TU/e research-data requirements

Research involving human participants or personal data must follow the current
TU/e ethical-review process. A high-risk personal-data activity may also require
a pre-DPIA or DPIA. Sharing or receiving personal or IP-protected data with an
external party may require a data agreement.

Use the current institutional guidance rather than copying policy details into
a project note:

- [TU/e RDM Framework Policy](https://rdm.tue.nl/docs/intro/tue-policy/)
- [Ethical approval](https://rdm.tue.nl/docs/before-research/ethical-approval/)
- [Privacy and Data Protection Impact Assessment](https://rdm.tue.nl/docs/privacy/privacy-impact-assessment/)
- [Data storage and sharing](https://rdm.tue.nl/docs/during-research/storage-and-sharing/)
- [Contact a TU/e Data Steward](https://rdm.tue.nl/docs/contact/)
